Overview & Research Questions

The study evaluated whether users could efficiently discover, filter, and act on products in SHEIN's shopping experience. The written report documents six sessions including a pilot. The final presentation identifies P1–P5 as the five post-pilot participants used for the formal results, so this page keeps the pilot separate.

Research questions: Can users quickly find products? Which interface elements create confusion? Do pop-ups interrupt task completion? Can users successfully filter by price, size, and color? How easy do tasks feel? How satisfied are users with the overall interface?

Pilot

P0 · February 17, 2026

The pilot ran about 16 minutes. It showed that Task 1 needed revised wording and Task 2 needed a complete rewrite before formal testing.

Formal usability testing

P1–P5

Five post-pilot participants completed moderated remote sessions. All had prior experience with SHEIN and were recruited through personal networks and social media.

Heuristic Evaluation

Poor system feedback

Menus and layouts varied across pages and could disorient users.

Lack of error prevention

The evaluation found few safeguards during checkout to prevent user errors.

Findings & Recommendations

01 · Fix the Filter System

Issue: filters returned no results or reset; users abandoned them. Recommendation: clearer labels, stable multi-select behavior, visible result count, and a “Clear all” control.

02 · Boost Quick-Add Visibility

Issue: the icon was overlooked and users entered product pages instead. Recommendation: a larger hover-state button, tooltip (“Add to Cart”), and stronger contrast.

03 · Surface the Favorites Feature

Issue: the heart icon was hard to find and new users encountered an account-creation wall. Recommendation: more prominent affordance, guest wishlisting, or a softer sign-in prompt after saving.

04 · Reduce Disruptive Interruptions

Issue: pop-ups, CAPTCHA, and unexpected navigation broke task flow. Recommendation: reduce overlay frequency, control redirects, and reserve CAPTCHA for suspicious activity.

how I would read the results

Limitations & Source Notes

  • Small convenience sample.
  • The pilot changed task wording before formal testing.
  • The written report and final presentation use slightly different participant framing.
  • The source documents record ASQ as a measure, but do not provide an aggregate ASQ score table in the final results section; no ASQ values are invented here.
  • Only three shopping tasks were evaluated.
