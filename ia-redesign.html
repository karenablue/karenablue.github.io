01

Membership is always top-level

Every benchmarked organization places membership information in primary navigation. The existing site exposed only “Reactivate” as a general membership entry point.

02

Programs are grouped under one umbrella

Comparable sites consolidate community work into one category; the existing site split it across Programs, Community Impact, and nested dropdowns.

03

Partner content is co-located

Peer sites group partnership and collaboration content together instead of distributing it across multiple top-level areas.

what held across methods

Cross-Method Synthesis

01

Membership belongs at the top level

Card sorting produced unanimous grouping of membership actions; benchmarking showed peer organizations expose membership in primary navigation.

02

Insider terminology is a barrier

Non-members paused or misplaced items when organizational jargon appeared. Plain-language context was needed alongside official terminology.

03

Service and program content share a mental model

Participants independently clustered programs, foundations, and initiatives together while the existing site fragmented them across multiple paths.

04

Structural depth slows everyone down

Card-sort hesitation and competitive patterns both supported a shallower architecture with two levels as the practical ceiling.

every change needed a reason

Design Rationale

Structural changeResearch basis
Membership elevated to primary navigationAll participants grouped membership content together; competitive analysis showed it as a top-level category.
Programs consolidated into “Programs & Initiatives”Participants grouped programs and partner initiatives together. The label is a research synthesis, not a direct consensus outcome.
News & Events reintroduced as a categoryCard sorting showed strong clustering of time-sensitive content such as press releases, conferences, and calendar items.
Navigation depth reducedTree testing showed faster completion when the structure was flatter; nested paths requiring 3+ clicks were eliminated.
Contact moved under Resources“Contact Us” had moderate card-sort agreement (66.7%), not enough to justify a standalone top-level item.
Plain-language context added for insider termsNon-Greek participants struggled with terminology such as “Grand Basileus.”
Sub-items ordered by user intentHigher-intent and broader-audience items appear first; “Become a Member” leads Membership, with Chapter Locator following.
Affiliates placed under Programs & InitiativesAffiliates had low card-sort consensus (44.4%); content review showed the groups function more like community/youth programs than membership pathways.

what this study does not prove

Limitations & Next Steps

  • Sample size of 9 participants limits generalizability.
  • No post-implementation testing was conducted.
  • Participants were recruited from a convenience sample.
  • Tree testing used a proposed structure, not a live site.

Key takeaway: Navigation problems in large organizations are rarely just interface problems. They are symptoms of structural misalignment between how an organization governs its content internally and how users form mental models of that content externally.
