Sigma Gamma Rho: rethinking the path to membership

Navigation that makes sense to members can confuse newcomers. I researched and redesigned Sigma Gamma Rho’s website navigation, preserving official titles while adding context for first-time visitors.

the problem, my contribution, and where it stands

Overview

The original website split membership actions across menus and used titles that assumed visitors already knew the organization. As the independent researcher and designer, I conducted a competitive review, card sorting, and tree testing, then designed a revised navigation structure, desktop and mobile menu states, and a clickable prototype.

Current status: the research evaluated a proposed text-only structure. The interface is a design proposal awaiting participant testing.

the findings beside the decisions they informed

Key evidence

Members recognized the leader; newcomers struggled

Evidence: in the proposed structure’s tree test, 4 of 4 members found the current leader, compared with only 1 of 5 non-members. Two non-members chose the board rather than the individual; two ended in About. This suggests the title needed context, though the small sample cannot isolate terminology from other navigation factors.

Decision: keep the official title and add a plain-language description. This preserves familiar terminology for members while explaining the role to newcomers.

Joining was easier to recognize

Evidence: all 9 card-sort participants grouped “Become a Member” with membership actions. In the proposed tree, all 8 who attempted the join task succeeded directly (7.1 seconds on average). The task wording closely matched the destination label, so this supports findability within that tree, not improvement over the original site.

Decision: make Membership a primary category and put “Become a Member” first, giving prospective members a visible starting point.

“Service & Impact” left programs ambiguous

Evidence: only 5 of 8 found community programs, and just 3 went directly there. All three misses selected a foundation. Card sorting grouped programs and foundations together (7 of 9 for National Programs).

Decision: use “Programs & Initiatives” for the shared category. This is my interpretation of the grouping; its wording still needs testing.

Other placement decisions and tradeoffs

News & Events reintroduced

Time-sensitive content clustered strongly: press, events, and news stayed together.

9 of 9 · Latest News, Upcoming Events

Depth capped at two levels

The original menus ran three levels deep (Affiliates and Partners), so I reduced nesting to make destinations visible sooner. The effect on task time needs testing.

3 levels before, 2 after

Contact moved under Resources

Agreement for "Contact Us" was moderate, not enough to justify a standalone top-level item.

Affiliates placed under Membership

Membership was the most common placement for Affiliates, though not a majority. I first placed it under Programs, then moved it so the structure follows the data.

4 of 9
Header alternatives and the selected pattern

I had already chosen a single-row ribbon in my full redesign. To check that choice rather than assume it, I went back and compared it with two alternative header patterns as greyscale wireframes in Figma, each with the Membership menu open.

Decision: I kept Option 1, the single-row ribbon with dropdowns and Join in the masthead.

Why Option 1: every section stays visible and one click from any page, which suits a two-level structure with six sections. Option 2’s persistent Join competed with the ribbon, and Option 3’s mega menu asked visitors to scan far more than the join task needed.

what was tested and what comes next

Evaluation

So far, the evidence covers the structure, not the interface. The tree test checked a proposed structure with 9 participants. The final design and its prototype have not been in front of users yet, so round 2 tests what changed.

Round 2 · Tree test

The final structure

At least 5 non-members. Join and find-a-chapter as controls, a retest of the two weak tasks (the current leader, community programs), and contacting the organization under Resources.

Round 2 · Moderated test

The prototype

3 to 5 first-time non-members on desktop and phone: start the join path, find who leads the organization, and find something it does in the community.

What I'll measure Round 1 baseline Round 2 target
Non-members finding the current leader 1 of 5 At least 4 of 5
Visitors finding community programs 5 of 8 At least 7 of 8
Placements resting on judgment 5 untested All five tree-tested

Targets are mine, set before any retest. Round 1 tested a proposed structure, round 2 tests the final design, and each result will be reported next to its baseline with the sample size.

Reflection

My familiarity with Sigma’s titles could hide a newcomer’s difficulty. I kept official titles and added helper descriptions, balancing member expectations with newcomer clarity. Those descriptions are in the prototype and awaiting testing.

methods, detailed results, and limitations

Supporting research

Original navigation and problem context

The site's main job is to start a relationship with people who want to join, yet joining sat outside the main navigation. Twelve top-level links competed for attention, membership actions were split across a "Members" menu and a utility bar, and some menus opened a menu inside a menu. Insider titles assumed the visitor already knew the organization.

Research approach and researcher positionality

Research question: why do prospective members struggle to find membership information, and what does the navigation reveal about the gap between how the organization thinks about its content and how visitors expect to find it?

Participation by phase 9 card-sort participants and 9 tree-test participants. Four people took part in both phases (2 undergraduate sorors and 2 non-members); the other five in each phase were different.
Card sort segments 6 sorors (3 undergraduate, 3 alumnae) and 3 non-members
Tree test segments 4 members (3 undergraduate, 1 alumna) and 5 non-members, one of whom belongs to another Divine Nine organization
Methods Competitive analysis of 3 Divine Nine (NPHC) sororities, open card sort (24 cards), tree test (10 tasks)

Exclusion: one additional card-sort session, submitted on May 29, 2025 after the study closed on May 27, was excluded from the analysis. Including it would not change the main findings: the join grouping stays unanimous, and National Programs agreement would be 70% instead of 77.8%.

Researcher positionality: I am a member of Sigma Gamma Rho, so its structure and titles are familiar to me. That familiarity can make a label feel clearer than it does to a first-time visitor. The card sort included three non-members, and one of them placed “International Grand Basileus” under About while all six sorors put it under Leadership; the tree test included five non-members, and finding the current leader was its weakest task. I recommended keeping official terms and explaining them in plain language.

Study limitations
  • A sample of 9 participants per phase limits how far the results generalize.
  • I did not tree-test the original site structure with the same tasks, so I can show how the proposed structure performs but not that it beats the old one.
  • The join task names the action it wants ("becoming a member") and the answer is labeled "Become a Member," so its 100% shows the label is findable, not that the structure is strong. The member versus non-member gap on the leader task is the more informative result.
  • Participants came from a convenience sample.
  • The tree test used a proposed structure, not a live site.
  • Four placements rest on judgment more than on the card sort: Notable Sigmas, Anti-Hazing Policy, International Corporate Headquarters, and Media Relations. Tree-test them, along with Affiliates under Membership (4 of 9, not a majority), before treating the structure as settled.
  • The final design and its prototype have not been tested with participants. The design's departures from the tested structure and the revised "Programs & Initiatives" label are the next things to test.

  • Membership is always top-level. Every benchmarked organization puts membership in primary navigation. The original site had a Join Sigma button in the utility bar and a Members menu, but no dedicated Membership category in the main row.
  • Programs are grouped under one umbrella. Comparable sites keep community work in one category. The original site split it across Programs, Community Impact, and nested dropdowns.
  • Partner content is co-located. Peer sites keep partnership content together instead of spreading it across several top-level areas.
