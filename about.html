the short version:

I like making complicated things make sense.

I'm Karena — a UX researcher, designer, and web creator who is usually most interested in the part of a digital experience that makes people stop, hesitate, backtrack, or ask, “Wait... where am I supposed to go?”

My background started in computer science, but I became much more interested in the human side of technology: how people understand systems, how language shapes navigation, and how design decisions either reduce friction or quietly create more of it.

That is what pulled me deeper into human–computer interaction, UX research, information architecture, and design. I still like building things — I just care a lot more now about whether the thing actually makes sense to the person using it.

favorite kind of problem “Why is this harder than it needs to be?”

things I keep coming back to

How I think about design

⌕

Understand before designing.

I want to know what people are trying to do, where they get stuck, and what the current experience is asking them to figure out on their own.

✦

Clarity beats cleverness.

Personality matters. So does delight. But if a person cannot tell what something means or what to do next, the clever part is not helping.

♡

Useful can still feel human.

I do not believe every professional interface needs to look sterile. Good hierarchy and accessibility can coexist with warmth and personality.

one more thing...

I like work that gives me something to investigate. The best projects, for me, usually start with a system that technically works but feels confusing, overly complicated, or disconnected from the people using it.

Give me the messy navigation, the contradictory feedback, the weird process, the information nobody knows where to put — that's usually where I get interested.
